# The three pilots, as they stand (critique against docs/craft/taste-checklist.md)

Written before the comps, so each comp starts from named problems, not a vague "make it nicer".

## #16 Timesheet (the owner's current bar)

- **Context.** A freelance designer's time tracker: one running timer, today's entries, a total. Calm, glanceable,
  money on the line.
- **First impressions.** Clean and legible, and it's the best of the forty, which is why it's the bar. It's also a
  white card of text: a status dot, a title, a big figure, a round pause button, four rows, a total.
- **World: none.** No window, no sidebar, no sense of the app: the card floats on the dot board.
- **Life: thin.** One violet button is the only colour besides a green dot. No client marks, no visual of the day (how
  the six hours split, where the gaps were), no sign the timer is running except the digits.
- **Hierarchy.** Good: the 1:42:37 figure leads, then the entry titles, then muted clients and money. Four levels.
- **Density.** Rows are 64px apart with two lines each: calm and right for a timer, but they're all alike.
- **Delivery.** The lesson (tabular figures) lands on the big figure and the column: the strongest moment we have.
- **Direction.** Keep the hierarchy and calm. Give it a world (the app's sidebar of clients or projects), a visual of
  the day (a timeline strip, the running block growing), client marks with colour, and a running state you can feel.

## #59 Layer menu (probe stage)

- **Context.** A design tool's layer panel with a context menu: a power user, fast hands, many times a day.
- **First impressions.** A wireframe of a design tool: a breadcrumb, four layer names as plain text, a violet square
  for a canvas, and the menu. 
- **World: a sketch of one.** The layers have no icons, no nesting, no visibility or lock toggles; the canvas has one
  flat shape and no artboard, no frame label, no selection handles worth the name; there's no toolbar.
- **Life: none.** Nothing is being designed. The canvas should hold a real piece of work (a card with an image and
  type) that the layers describe.
- **Hierarchy and density.** The menu is fine; everything around it is so empty that the menu is the only real object.
- **Delivery.** The probe stage works; the frames it plays show this wireframe, so the craft of the moment sits on a
  screen nobody would use.
- **Direction.** A real design tool's panel: a toolbar, layers with type icons and nesting, an artboard holding a real
  card (image, headline, button) with its selection drawn, a properties hint on the right. The menu stays the subject.

## #57 Inbox

- **Context.** A team's email inbox, the second most-used surface of a working day: dense, scanned, triaged.
- **First impressions.** Three messages as three blocks of text: name, subject, preview, time. Nothing else.
- **World: none.** No folders, no search, no toolbar, no reading pane: a list floating on the board.
- **Life: none.** No avatars, no unread state, no labels, no attachments, no selection, no star or pin. Every row is
  the same three grey lines.
- **Hierarchy.** Names medium, subjects regular, previews muted: three flat levels with nothing to scan by.
- **Density.** Three rows at 85px is a phone's inbox drawn on a desktop; a real inbox shows eight to twelve.
- **Delivery.** The lesson (the faded @mention) is a 13px word in the third row of a wireframe: it can't land.
- **Direction.** A real mail client: folders and labels on the left, a dense list with avatars, unread weight and dots,
  labels, attachment marks, a selected row, and the reading pane beginning on the right. The muted preview line with
  the @mention lives in that dense list, where fading it really costs you.
