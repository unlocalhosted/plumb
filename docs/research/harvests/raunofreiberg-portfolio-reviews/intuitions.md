# Rauno Freiberg's intuitions, sorted by kind of seeing

From one thread of 2026-10-19, read against each page on 2026-11-26. He wrote 47 replies: 40 open a review of a
site (39 sites; one of the 40 is a one-line joke), 4 follow up on a review, and 3 are asides. All 47 are listed in
`reviews.json`. Every point is there with what the page shows and how he got there; the numbers below (such as 21.3)
are those points. This file sorts them by the kind of seeing they need, and says what plumber has for each.

120 points: 37 about shape, 21 about craft detail, 41 about behaviour, 21 about content and story. Each point has one
of those four labels, and the labels are ours. The tables below group points by what he was looking at, so a table
under "Shape" can hold a point labelled behaviour or craft: ten of them do.

## How much of this we saw, and three corrections

This file was audited on 2026-11-26, after the owner asked whether it could be trusted. Three things were wrong and
are corrected here and in `reviews.json` (`audit.py` holds every change).

- **Much of it was never seen by us.** For 27 points we read the page or a copy of it. For 40 we saw the thing in his
  recording or picture, or in ours. For 53 we have only his sentence. Of the 41 points about behaviour, 27 are in that
  last group. The earlier text described several of those as if we had watched them. Each point now says which group
  it is in and what exactly we did not see.
- **Plumber was described from memory.** The earlier text said plumber could not see relations between things, and
  that eight of his points were checks it already had. Plumber's catalogue has 306 checks, and it was read in full for
  this audit. Six of his points are the same judgement as an existing check. Twenty-four more have a neighbour: the
  same idea on another case. Three run against an existing check (see below).
- **The boxes on the live sites do not hold up.** Of 98 boxes, 14 mark one clear thing that is on the page today.
  The others mark a fault that has since been removed (17), something we never saw (29), a place taken from an older
  copy of the page (27), a movement or a press that a box cannot show (27), a whole section (25), or the same area
  as another point (21). A box can be in several of those groups. The last section says why.

## His methods

These are his methods, and they matter more than any single point.

- **He watches his own hand.** He reached for zoom before reading (1.2), reached for a cube that would not turn
  (39.1), clicked a heading that looked like a link (21.3), moved the window to find a comfortable position (13.2).
  The hand reports the fault before he can name it.
- **He uses the thing as he really would.** He opens every project in a tab to compare them (18.1), presses each
  menu entry (1.4), reads an article to its end (42.1), finishes the game the page sets (41.6).
- **He compares a promise with what follows.** The share picture against the site (12.1). The look against the
  motion (36.1, 38.1). A hover against a click (40.4). An underline against a link (21.3).
- **He takes things away in his head** and keeps the page that is better (7.2, 25.1, 30.1).
- **He counts what each line or picture tells him** against the room it takes (24.1, 21.2, 13.1).
- **He draws the boxes.** For one page he outlined each block, wrote its size and proportion on it, and judged the
  arrangement of rectangles (40.1). This is the idea this whole line of work started from, done by hand.
- **He hands over the test and not the bug.** Set scrollbars to always show (8.1). Click back and forth fast (9.4).
- **He knows the field and the maker.** He knows what was in fashion and when (3.1, 3.3, 7.1), and who built the
  page (11.1).

explain how to read the thing beside them. The rest is advice about the person's work, which a model could give and
no measurement can.

## Six lessons for the engine

1. **"Looks like it means" against "means" holds up, for about a quarter of what he said.** Twenty-seven of the 120
   points are a case of one of the five comparisons. Loud against what matters is the largest (13), then alike
   against same role (8), enclosed against scope (3), linked against related (2), near against belongs (1). Another
   21 are shape in a wider sense that the five do not cover: edges and proportion that nearly agree (5), one language
   across a page, a site and its surfaces (7), and the kind of page and what it promises (9). The sorting is ours,
   and each point's group is in `reviews.json`.
2. **The three-way judgement is his, said once.** Exact is fine, far apart is a choice, nearly is the fault. He says
   it about one page (40.1): more clutter would be interesting, this is a middle ground. Twice more he says commit or
   drop, about a game (7.3) and a desktop idea (38.3). Reading it into his other points is ours. Plumber already
   judges this way inside one list.
3. **Loudness needs meaning.** Seven points turn on what matters most, which only the words can say.
4. **The page is wider than the page.** Tab icon, share picture, browser bars, and the other pages of the site. Ten
   points sit there, where plumber does not look.
5. **Conditions come first.** Thirteen ways of looking produced 27 of the points. Several need no new check, only
   an old check run under a new condition.
6. **A third of it is behaviour over time.** No still reading of a page reaches those 41 points, and we have seen
   only 14 of them ourselves.
7. **An expert and a rule can disagree.** Three of his points run against a check plumber has. Each time the rule is
   right in general and he is right on that page, and what decides is one more reading.

- The sorting into levels is ours, and so is every sentence under "how he got there". He wrote one line; we inferred

- 53 points rest on his sentence alone, and 53 were not checked on today's page. The two groups overlap but are not
  the same.
- No check of plumber's was run on any of these pages. Every "plumber has this" is a match of judgements on paper.

## Why the boxes on the live sites miss his points

His points were loaded into the judging page as boxes over each live site. Looked at on the live sites, most of the
boxes do not show what he meant. A box is one rectangle, taken from one still picture of one moment. His points are
mostly about something else:

- **A state the page passes through.** An opening screen, a hover, selected text, the footer scrolled under a fixed
  menu, a very wide window. The live page at rest is in none of those states. On one site the boxes for two photos
  float over an opening screen where the photos are not shown yet.
- **A comparison between two things.** Headings against links, one tile against the next, the share picture against
  the page. One rectangle cannot hold a comparison.
- **A page that has changed since.** Seventeen boxes sit where a fault used to be.
- **Something we never saw.** Twenty-nine boxes are our guess at where his sentence applies.

What replaces the box is built for one site, Sam Limby's (`scenes.py`), and the owner has agreed to that much. Each point is shown as he met it. The page is
put in the state he was in. The mark is tied to the things by name, two of them when the point is a comparison. Where
the page has changed, his view stands beside today's. Where we have not seen it, there is no mark, only his sentence
and the words "not seen yet".
